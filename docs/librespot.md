# Spotify (librespot)

Q2 Pod plays Spotify through a librespot port that runs from the microSD card. Spotify Premium is required. After a one-time Spotify Connect pairing from a phone, the Q2 browses and plays on its own over Wi-Fi; the phone can still pick the music over Spotify Connect. Either way the Q2 streams and decodes it itself.

## Port

The port lives in [DiamondBond/q2-librespot](https://github.com/DiamondBond/q2-librespot), a librespot fork. Its branch `shanlingq2` is v0.8.0 plus an IPv4 fallback for discovery, because the Q2's kernel has no IPv6. Its `contrib/shanlingq2/` holds the build, the card launcher and the README.

- **Release:** [q2-librespot's releases](https://github.com/DiamondBond/q2-librespot/releases/latest) ship the prebuilt card files as `q2-librespot-*.zip`, unzipped to the card's root.
- **On the card:** the binary, its launcher and its sink go in the card's `.spotify` folder, with the saved login in `.spotify/cache/`. Q2 Pod starts it; see the [user guide](https://github.com/DiamondBond/q2-pod/releases/latest/download/Q2-Pod-User-Guide.pdf) to install it.
- **Build:** since `q2-v1.7`, librespot is built **hard-float** for the X2000's FPU, with the stock binaries' ABI (`-mfp64`, NaN-2008). It is linked dynamically against the Q2's own glibc 2.28 through `/lib/ld-linux-mipsn8.so.1`, the way Q2 Pod's helpers are. The libraries it links against come from the V1.36 firmware ZIP's rootfs. Earlier releases were static soft-float (musl).
- **Audio:** goes through the stock `aplay` to `plughw:0,0`, the CS43131 headphone DAC, started only while librespot plays (its subprocess backend), so local music can open the DAC whenever Spotify is paused. With Bluetooth or a USB DAC as the output, Q2 Pod names the device and volume in `/tmp/q2-librespot.out` and `aplay.sh` runs Q2 Pod's `q2video -s` there instead, at hciplayer's soft volume ([internals](internals.md#spotify)).

**It plays.** On the device, librespot appears in Spotify Connect, the phone's session authenticates, and audio comes out of the headphones at 160 kbit/s. Hard-float decoding takes about 3% of a core, against about 31% for the earlier soft-float build.

**Playback doesn't start.** Spotify refuses librespot the key that unlocks a track's audio for some accounts, mostly newer ones, though older ones too ([librespot#1649](https://github.com/librespot-org/librespot/issues/1649)). The account signs in and the Q2 shows up in Spotify Connect, but every track fails, and `.spotify/spot.log` on the card has `error audio key 0 1` for each one. Q2 Pod then shows "Spotify refused this account" and restarts librespot, which would otherwise skip through the whole list. Neither the Q2 nor librespot can fix it. Another Premium account (a family member's, say) may play, and Spotify's own headless client, Soloist, runs only on ARM and x86 Linux, macOS and Windows, not on the Q2's MIPS processor.

## Q2 Pod

**Streaming → Spotify** opens a browser: Now Playing (the cover, title, artist and album, a moving progress bar and the play state, in Now Playing's layout), Liked Songs, Playlists, Albums, Artists, Search, Queue, Playback Settings (shuffle, repeat), Account (sign out) and Downloads, which holds Downloaded Tracks (before v1.10, Downloaded Tracks sits on the menu beside it). It needs q2-librespot v1.3 or later, Spotify Premium and Wi-Fi for new tracks. Downloaded Tracks replays previously played, completely cached tracks without Wi-Fi. Without a saved login it asks for a one-time pairing: pick Q2 in the Spotify app on a phone on the same Wi-Fi, and the page turns into the menu. With an older add-on, Now Playing still works from the phone and the page asks for the update. Q2 Pod treats librespot as a source of its own playback, as it treats Internet Radio's q2video:

- **Start:** only from the row, once per boot; until then nothing Spotify runs or polls. Idle 30 s after playing (2½ min if it never played) with the browser shut, or **Streaming → Configuration → Stop Streaming Services**, it stops; the row starts it again.
- **Cache:** the launcher caps the audio cache at `.spotify/.cache-size` (`2048M`, written by Streaming → Configuration's Spotify Cache, 512 MB to 8 GB), read at each librespot start; `2G` when the file is missing or malformed.
- **Power:** while it plays, standby, auto power-off and the DAC's power-off are held off; the screen still turns off.
- **DAC:** set up as stock's AirPlay receiver sets it (`config_outputchannel`, as a headset insert does, then PCM mode, unmuted and the Q2's volume).
- **Keys:** Play/Pause and the side buttons control Spotify while it was the last thing played; the wheel is the Q2's volume, which is Spotify's.
- **Hand-over:** casting stops local music; starting a local track pauses Spotify first.

The fork adds three options for this: `--status-file`, the player's state, track, position and cover for the page, `--control-socket`, the commands from the keys, and `--browse-socket`, the browser's requests (below). How they fit together is in [internals.md](internals.md#spotify).

**Downloads** is a persistent hub for Downloaded Playlists, Downloaded Tracks, live progress and Storage Management. **Download Playlist** and **Download Liked Songs** preserve names, source order and duplicate positions. Partial collections remain browsable; playback skips missing entries without fetching online. Controls offer **Update Download**, **Retry Failed**, **Pause**, **Resume** and **Cancel Download**. Updates and resuming after reboot are manual; repeated requests do not duplicate jobs. Collections queue sequentially.

Explicit downloads use protected storage under `.spotify/cache/offline/audio/<account>/`, separate from the evictable playback cache. The Spotify Cache setting caps temporary playback storage and cannot evict protected downloads. Existing cached tracks remain available without invented playlist grouping: download that playlist again. Storage Management selects multiple tracks/playlists or clears all music after showing a count and confirmation. Removing a playlist keeps shared audio; removing tracks warns about incomplete playlists. Clearing downloads preserves login/settings; signing out removes login and offline music. Keep `.spotify/cache` when updating.

Collection controls require matching firmware and a backend advertising the saved-download capabilities (`q2-v1.10` or later). Older backends retain supported flat-list actions and an update explanation; older firmware's original operations still work with the new backend. See the [shared download UX/protocol contract](downloads.md) for examples, job states, removal semantics and Deezer/Qobuz guidance.

## Browsing protocol (Q2SPOT/1)

The fork's `--browse-socket /tmp/q2-librespot.browse.sock` serves Q2 Pod's Spotify browser. Older backends lack it; Q2 Pod then keeps the Connect Now Playing page and asks for the add-on to be updated.

The browser integration uses q2-librespot `shanlingq2` (`q2-v1.6` or later, Q2SPOT/1). Card updates replace `.spotify/librespot`, `.spotify/run` and `.spotify/aplay.sh`; preserve `.spotify/cache` to keep the paired login and complete downloads, and `.spotify/.cache-size` to keep the chosen cache size. Signing out deletes that account's saved audio, keys and artwork.

**Transport.** A Unix stream socket. One request per connection: the client writes the request and shuts down its write side; the backend answers and closes. Requests are at most 8 KiB, responses at most 64 KiB. The client gives up after 15 s.

**Framing.** Text lines ending in `\n`, fields separated by tabs. The first line of a request and of a response is `Q2SPOT/1`. In every field, the bytes `%`, tab, LF and CR are written as `%25`, `%09`, `%0A` and `%0D`; any other byte, UTF-8 included, is literal. A `%` not followed by two hex digits is malformed. Fields are opaque: IDs, URIs and cursors are passed back exactly as received, never rebuilt from displayed text.

Request, after the version line, one line:

    <id>	<op>	<arg>…

`id` is the client's decimal request number, echoed back so a late reply can be dropped. Response, after the version line:

    <id>	<status>	<message>	<cursor>
    <kind>	<uri>	<title>	<subtitle>	<context>	<position>
    …

The header is followed by 0 to 50 rows. `message` is one line of English for the screen (at most 120 bytes), usually empty on `ok`; capabilities and downloads use it for version or progress. `cursor`, when not empty, fetches the next page when passed as the op's last argument; it is at most 1 KiB. A page may hold fewer than 50 rows to stay within 64 KiB; its cursor then continues after the last row sent. `title` and `subtitle` are display text, at most 255 bytes each, cut on a character boundary; URIs and cursors are never cut.

Card apps (`patch/addon.c`) take three `message` values of an `ok` list response as instructions, not text. `refresh` asks the firmware to ask again every 2 s while the list is on screen; after an action on the list it is asked again at once, after the message's toast, so a choice row should leave the message empty for its mark to move straight away. Whenever a list's rows are replaced while its level stays (a `refresh` reply with other rows, the level asked again after an action, a `field`'s text or a `screen` row's run, Back to it), the selection stays on the row it was on, which the firmware knows by its kind, uri and context: a row added or removed above it doesn't move the selection to a neighbour, and a new title or detail doesn't matter. When several rows are equal in all three, the one at the old position stays selected if it is still there, else the first of them is. When the row is gone the selection keeps its position; so does a row past the first page of a list with more to come, selected once its page is there, and a section swiped back to. There is no capability word: older firmware goes by position alone. `pages` with a cursor asks it to fetch the following pages itself, each with the list's arguments and the newest cursor last, as More… would, and append them to one list, up to 10 responses (500 rows); it continues while each response says `pages` and has a cursor, and ends at a **More…** row otherwise. Any other message with a cursor shows **More…**. Firmware lists `pages` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps` when it does this; older firmware ignores the word and keeps More…. `sections`, on a first-level response, makes each `list` row a section (one with an empty uri is left out): the firmware shows the first section's list in its place, titled with its name and, when there are two or more, its position (`Albums 2/3`), and a sideways swipe moves to the next or previous section ([internals.md](internals.md#card-apps)). At most 8 sections are taken, the rest left out, and a section whose context doesn't fit is left out too; a swipe back returns a section paged by `page` rows to its first window. Firmware lists `sections` in the caps file when it does this; older firmware shows the rows as an ordinary list, so the sections stay reachable as rows.

Row fields:

- `kind`: `track`, `album`, `artist`, `playlist`, `account`, `setting` or `op`.
- `uri`: the item's Spotify URI (for `setting`, its name; for `op`, an op name).
- `title`, `subtitle`: what to show (a track's subtitle is its artists, an album's its artist, a playlist's its owner).
- `context`, `position`: for a playable track, `play <context> <position> <uri>` (the row's own URI last) starts it in its list, so the next track follows from Spotify's own context (even past the visible page) and a duplicated playlist entry starts at the copy chosen. An empty context means the track plays on its own: `play <uri>`.

Card apps (`patch/addon.c`) have row kinds of their own besides these. `progress` draws a bar, for an app's sync or download progress: `title` is the text on its left and `subtitle` the text on its right, on one line over a flat bar, and `position` is how much of the bar is filled, in permille (decimal, 0 to 1000; a number outside that range is clamped to it, and text that is not a number counts as 0). `uri` and `context` are empty. The row is inert: a press does nothing, and neither does a held Play/Pause. An app sends `progress` rows only when the word `progress` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`, and `info` rows otherwise (the title and subtitle as plain text, `position` empty): firmware without the word does not know the kind and takes `position` as the op a held Play/Pause sends, so a `progress` row at 47% would send `470` to the app as an op. Progress rows belong in a `refresh` list, which the firmware asks again every 2 s, so the bars move; a `pages` list is asked once.

A card app's op `stop` is reserved: an `action` row whose `uri` is exactly `stop`, or a row whose held-Play/Pause op (`position`) is exactly `stop`, means the app is ending. Once the app answers it `ok`, the firmware counts the app as stopped (it no longer keeps standby off or Wi-Fi on for it) and leaves its page; it sends no signal, so the app ends itself after replying.

A card app may also end itself when nothing has used it for a while, its launcher's loop over and its pid file `/tmp/q2-<id>` removed, even with its page still open. The page's next request for a level (a row that opens a list, a `refresh` list's next ask, the level asked again after an action or a `screen` row's run) then starts the app as its row would and waits for its socket as at a first start: a level not yet shown waits at "Starting <name>…", and a `refresh` level on show stays shown. The page does that once: an app still not there 10 s after that start shows "<name> isn't running" with **Retry**, and the page starts it again by itself only after it has answered. An action row's request, a held-Play/Pause op and a `field`'s text never start it: an action toasts "<name> isn't running", and its level, asked again after it, does the starting, except after a `stop` op. The firmware doesn't know why an app ended: an end the app makes for any reason but a `stop` op (a stop on its own web page, an action row whose op has another name) is taken for an idle one, and its open page's next request for a level starts it again, a `refresh` level's within 2 s. Only a `stop` op says an end was asked for, and the page closes with it. There is no capability word: on older firmware the page shows "<name> isn't running" at once, and Retry starts the app.

A card app can show a cover beside its rows when the word `art` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`. It leaves a JPEG at `/tmp/q2-<id>.art/<key>.jpg`, one file per row: `<key>` is the FNV-1a 32 hash of the row's `uri`, a tab and its `context` (the values, before the `%` escapes), written as 8 lowercase hex digits. A cover is at most 128 px either way, and is shown 64 px square; a larger one, or one whose size can't be read, counts as no cover. Write each file whole and rename it into place, so the firmware never reads half of one. The app cleans `/tmp/q2-<id>.art` itself. Covers are decided when a list is drawn: fetch them before replying, or reply `refresh`. Each reply of a `refresh` list on screen, one the same as the last too, has its covers counted again, and when their number has changed the list is drawn again with the selected row kept: a cover written after the reply shows within 2 s, and one removed goes. Only the number is compared, so a cover written as another row's is removed is not noticed until the list is drawn again; and a `refresh` list that More… has added a page to is counted only when its reply changes. There is no capability word: older firmware counts only when the reply itself has changed, so there an app changes a row's text for a late cover to show. A list with any cover is drawn with covers, and a row without one keeps its room empty; a list with a `progress` row never has covers, and the first page of a `pages` list decides.

A `page` row moves a long list to another window of it: `title` is what it shows (Next page, Previous page), `context` a cursor of the same list (empty for its start), and `uri` `prev` for an earlier window (its last row is selected) or `next` for a later one (its first row past Previous page is selected, and past a Search row left as a row, and a Sort row after it). A `page` row's cursor has to fit with the list's own arguments in 400 bytes, past which the press does nothing. The firmware asks for the list's own op and arguments with that cursor as the last argument, none if it is empty, and shows the reply in the list's place, so Back leaves the list. Send it only when the word `window` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`; firmware without it doesn't know the kind.

A `sort` row is a `list` row whose list is a sort choice, such as a page of sort orders: `uri` is the op it opens, `context` the op's argument and `title` the page's name, and a press opens it. Where the list's first row is a `search` row shown as the title bar's magnifying glass (a theme with a title bar), a `sort` row sent right after it, and not as the list's last row, is shown as stock's sort icon left of the glass instead, and a tap on it opens the same level. Anywhere else it is a list row with a chevron. Send it only when the word `sort` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`, and a `list` row otherwise: firmware without the word doesn't know the kind.

Card apps also get one level of their own from the firmware: `goto`, `album` or `artist` then a song's path, the first level of the app's page when Go to Album or Go to Artist is used on one of its songs (under its `.<id>/` folder) and its `q2app` holds `goto=1`. The app answers with that album's or artist's own list. Firmware that sends it lists `goto` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`.

A `switch` row is an on/off setting drawn as the settings pages' own rows are: `title` beside the switch (On or Off as a value under Classic), on while `subtitle` is exactly `on` and off otherwise; the subtitle is never shown as text. A press is an `action` row's: `<uri>` then `<context>` when not empty, the app answering `ok` with the toast text or none, and the list asked again so the switch moves. Send `switch` rows only when the word `switch` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`, and an `action` row with `On` or `Off` as its subtitle otherwise: older firmware doesn't know the kind and a press does nothing.

A `field` row is a value the user types on the page, such as a sign-in page's user name or password: `title` is the field's name (`Username:`), `subtitle` what the row shows under it, `uri` the op that takes the text, `context`, when not empty, an argument sent before the text, and `position`, when the word `fieldtext` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`, the text the box opens with, for a value the user edits rather than types again (an address; never send a password there): the caret at its end, nothing selected, and under 64 bytes, as any text sent (a longer one opens, but OK sends nothing); held Play/Pause does nothing on a `field` row. Older firmware takes `position` as a held-Play/Pause op, so send it only with `fieldtext`. The app chooses the subtitle: the name typed, `***` for a password, or a hint while it is empty; the firmware never shows the typed text in a row, a title or anywhere else once the box has closed. A press opens the T9 box, its hint the row's `title`. OK with text sends one request, `<uri>` then `<context>` when not empty then the text as the last argument (escaped as any field); OK with the box empty, or Return, sends nothing, and so does a text of 64 bytes or more, which is never cut: the firmware toasts "Too long" and the page stays as it was. The reply is the level's own, in its place: no level is pushed and nothing is titled with the text, and the level's Loading… page shows until it comes. An `ok` reply's rows are drawn as the level's rows, its title kept and its selected row kept, so the app answers with the page again, the row's subtitle now the new value; its `message` is a list reply's (`refresh`, `pages`; a next page is asked with the level's own op). Any other status, or no answer, shows the level's error page, the message as its title; so does no socket while the app is starting or restarting ("<name> isn't running"), where a level's own request would wait. Every later request for the level — its Retry, Back from a deeper level, a `refresh` — sends the level's own op and arguments, never the field's request again, so a refused password is never sent twice and the app keeps what was typed itself, answering the level's op with the page as it now stands. Send `field` rows only when the word `field` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`: older firmware doesn't know the kind and a press does nothing, so an app falls back on its own flow there (a `search` row's text opens a `search` level, titled with the text).

A card app whose `q2app` holds `stop=0` gets no **Stop <name>** row on its page: a level that is slow to load (3 s) shows Loading… alone, and an error page shows **Retry** and **Cancel**, where Cancel does what Return does (back to the level below, the level asked again, or off the app's page from its first level) and the app keeps running. The firmware's Stop row never ends it; Retry's restart, USB storage mode and the App Store still can, so it must handle a TERM. Any other value, or none, keeps the Stop row; older firmware ignores the key. There is no capability word: an app needs no fallback.

A card app whose `q2app` holds `more=<op>`, or `more=<op> <title>`, takes Now Playing's ⋯ while its queue plays: a queue its `.play` started (a folder, a playlist or a `urls` queue) with no other queue loaded since. Pressing ⋯, or the hold menu's More…, then opens the app's page at `op` with no arguments, titled `title` (the app's name without one), instead of stock's ⋯ page, whose rows (Playlists, Quality Choice) mean nothing for an app's queue; an app page already open closes first, and Back from the new one returns to Now Playing. The level is an ordinary first level, so its reply may say `sections`, `pages` or `refresh`. `op` is 1 to 15 bytes with no space, else ⋯ stays stock's, as it does once the app's binary is gone from the card; the value is read up to 47 bytes. A queue the user loads (from the Library, or stock reloading the app's), and every queue before the app's first `.play` since power-on, keeps stock's ⋯. Firmware that does this lists `more` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`; older firmware ignores the key.

A card app's `/tmp/q2-<id>.play` starts a queue each time its text changes, so the app may leave it in place when it stops: started again, the same file plays nothing, and `/tmp/q2-<id>.playing` goes on following the queue. One already there when Q2 Pod starts (after a crash, or back from Rockbox) isn't played again either; a new seq plays. There is no capability word: older firmware plays a `.play` left there again when it starts.

A card app whose songs would only clutter a listening history, such as live recordings that run into one another, can put `scrobble=0` in its `q2app`. A song under its `.<id>/` folder then still counts as a play but stays out of `.scrobbler.log`; a song it names elsewhere on the card logs as usual, and so does any other value of the key or none. There is no capability word: older firmware ignores the key. A user's own edit lasts until an update of the app replaces the file.

A card app's row is on **Streaming**, and a game's (`game=1`, below) in Library's **Games**, unless its `q2app` holds `row=`: `row=library` puts the row on Library's **Apps** page (Library → Apps; up to 1.8.8 it was a row of the Library itself, above Games, and the same line works on both); `row=games` in Library's Games; `row=streaming` on Streaming. Where the row is doesn't change what a press does: an app's row opens its page, the level `menu` it serves on its socket, and a game's row starts it over the whole screen, from any of the three. So an app with a menu of its own that works on the user's music, or a tool, goes in Library → Apps with one line (q2-mixtape's `q2app` is `name=Mixtape`, `search=…`, `more=…` and `row=library`), a game that has a menu in Games (`row=games` without `game=1`), and a full-screen tool in Apps (`game=1` with `row=library`). Apps' and Games' rows show the name alone, no icon. A Streaming row waits for Smart Wi-Fi's link before the app starts; an Apps or Games row opens at once, so an app there must not count on the link in its first seconds (an app with a page has the radio back because it runs; a game doesn't wake it). Any other word is as no `row`. There is no capability word: older firmware ignores the key and lists the app on Streaming, or in Games for a game, so an app needs no fallback. Once an app is installed the App Store says where its row is ("Mixtape is ready in Library's Apps").

A card app that never uses the network, such as one that plays the card's own files, can put `wifi=0` in its `q2app`. Smart Wi-Fi (System settings → Power, which turns the radio off while nothing needs the network) then doesn't count it: the radio is neither kept on nor woken because the app runs or because its page is open. Put such an app's row in Library's Apps or Games (`row=library` or `row=games`, above): Streaming's page wakes the radio whatever it lists, and a Streaming row waits for the link before its app starts. The app's QR code page (a `remote` row's) still counts as a network page while it is open. Any other value, or none, leaves the app counted. There is no capability word: older firmware ignores the key and keeps the radio on while the app runs, so an app needs no fallback.

`row=network` puts an app's row in **System settings → Network**, after stock's DLNA, AirPlay and Wi-Fi File Transfer, by name, with the 52 px `icon.png` first: the place for an app that connects the Q2 rather than plays, such as a VPN. A press opens its page as from Streaming. Firmware that does this lists `network` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`; older firmware lists the app on Streaming.

An app can draw its icon for a theme: `icon-<theme>.png` and `icon-40-<theme>.png`, `<theme>` the theme's name in lower case, a space becoming a dash (`minimal`, `modern`, `classic`, `classic-modern`), come before `icon.png` and `icon-40.png` while that theme is on, in the same size order, and are read again at each scan, so a theme change shows the next time the page opens. Minimal greys card apps' icons and draws its settings rows' icons as grey discs, so an app can give it one drawn that way. Firmware without this takes the plain names.

`wifi=1` makes an app follow the Wi-Fi link: the firmware starts it when the link comes up (at boot too, without a visit to its row) and stops it once the link has been gone 10 seconds, so a short drop or a DHCP renew leaves it running. It doesn't keep the radio on: Smart Wi-Fi may still turn Wi-Fi off once nothing else needs it, which stops the app 10 s later, and turning Wi-Fi back on starts it again. If the user stops it (its page's **Stop** row, the `stop` op, **Stop Streaming Services**) or it ends by itself, it stays stopped until Wi-Fi is back after being gone 10 s or more, so an app should not end its own loop on a network blip. Its own row still starts it by hand. Firmware that does this lists `wifi` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`; older firmware ignores the key, so the app is started from its row.

A card app can put an icon in the status bar while it runs, such as a VPN's while it is connected, when the word `bar` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`. It writes `bar=` in its status file `/tmp/q2-<id>.state`: `bar=1` shows `bar.png` from its folder, `bar=<word>` (1 to 8 of `a-z0-9`) shows `bar-<word>.png`, and `bar=0`, nothing or any other value shows none. So an app with states ships one icon each and switches between them: a VPN writes `bar=on` (`bar-on.png`, connected) and `bar=off` (`bar-off.png`, greyed). An icon is at most 40 px wide and 20 px high; a larger one, or one whose size can't be read, counts as none. The firmware looks the file up when the value changes. The icon shows left of Wi-Fi, at its own width and centred on Wi-Fi's height, while the app runs and its status file's `bar=` names one: the firmware reads that file every half second, as it reads `hold=`. With several such apps, the first by name shows. The icon shows only where the bar has room for it before the clock: in a Battery mode that shows the number, the battery may turn to the plain icon to make room, and where even that leaves too little, the app's icon isn't shown. Make the icon white on transparent, as the bar's own icons are: Light Mode turns it to the bar's ink. Older firmware ignores both.

A card app whose `q2app` holds `game=1` is a game, listed in Library's Games (unless `row` says where, above) and run over the whole screen with the input as text datagrams on its socket ([internals](internals.md#q2-shelf), Card games); firmware that does this lists `game` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`. One that also holds `audio=1` gets the audio output handed over before it starts (the player stopped, the headphone output readied), the output device and a soft output's volume as its arguments, and Play/Pause and the side buttons as key datagrams; its idle close is its own. The arguments are q2video's (`patch/video.c`), and so are the duties: `usb` names the USB DAC's card in `/proc/asound/cards` (`N [id]: USB-Audio - name`, so `plughw:N,0`); hciplayer lets go of the PCM, and mutes the DAC, a moment after the firmware's stop, so the open is retried for a couple of seconds (5 s on Bluetooth and USB, and up to 5 s more at `snd_pcm_set_params` off the jack, since bluealsa claims its PCM there, busy until then) and, on the jack, the DAC's mute and PCM mode are cleared after it (`/dev/shanling_dac`, ioctls `0xc0044d1f` and `0xc0044d1b` with an int 0) and the mute set to 1 again when the game closes the PCM; `hNN` is a volume the DAC applies, to show at most; Mono Audio and Swap L/R are `/tmp/q2mono` and `/tmp/q2swap`, an int each, 1 when on. q2-groovebox's `src/audio.c` and `src/platform_q2.c` do all of it. Firmware that does this lists `audio`; older firmware ignores the key, and the game then finds the PCM busy.

A game can draw its own menus as the theme draws the built-in games' pop-ups: the environment variable `Q2_POPUP` it starts with is `<style> <box> <frame> <title> <text> <pick> <pick_text>`, each colour `RRGGBB` as the screen shows it, in Light Mode too. `style` is `minimal` (a dark box with a hairline frame, the rows in capitals, a dot beside the chosen one), `modern` (a rounded dark box, the chosen row on an accent bar) or `classic` (a rounded panel: `box` is its paper and `frame` its outline and the line under its title strip, then 64 more colours, the title strip's 32 rows and the chosen row's 32, a gradient each). `text` is an unchosen row's, `pick` the dot, the bar or the band's middle, and `pick_text` the chosen row's text. The firmware's own pop-up is 281 px wide with 32 px rows (`patch/games.c` `popup`). More fields may follow in later firmware, after a space: ignore them. Without the variable (older firmware) the game keeps its own look; q2-fidget's `fidget.c` reads it.

A `screen` row runs the app's own binary over the whole screen, as a game's row runs a game: a film in a list of films, say, played by the app that lists it. A press starts `.<id>/q2-<id>` once more, beside the instance that serves the page, with the row in its environment: `Q2_URI` is the row's `uri` and `Q2_CONTEXT` its `context`, each as the app sent it with its escapes undone, and empty for an empty field. Neither is set when the binary starts any other way (its launcher's start, a game's row), so their presence tells this run from the app's own. The rest is a game's, above, by the app's `q2app`, which needs no `game=1`: the input as text datagrams on `/tmp/q2-<id>.input.sock`, which the run binds (the wheel's travel, the keys and touches; Return held ends it), `Q2_POPUP`, the idle close, and what `/tmp/q2-<id>.dim`, `audio=2` and `gameview=1` add ([internals](internals.md#q2-shelf), Card games). `audio=1` there gives the app's screen rows the sound, as it gives a game: the player stopped and the output readied before the start, the output device as its first argument (`plughw:0,0` on the headphone jack, `plug:bluealsa` or `usb`) and, on Bluetooth or a USB DAC, the volume as its second (`NN`, or `hNN` to show only), Play/Pause and the side buttons as key datagrams, and an idle close of its own; without it the run has no arguments and the music plays on. The row has no chevron, and a held Play/Pause on it still sends its `position` as an op. One runs at a time: a press does nothing while a card game is still running behind Now Playing, and a run that can't start toasts why, as a game's row does. End a run on a key's or a touch's release, not its press: what comes after the run is no longer the run's. The firmware eats the release of a Return or a Centre still down as the run ends, which the page would otherwise take as back and as a press of the selected row; it eats no other, so Play/Pause's release reaches the music and starts or pauses it. A touch's lift after the run presses no row of the page, which never saw its press. When the run ends, however it ends, and the app's page is still open, the firmware asks the page's top level again, so the app can show what the run changed (a film's place, a watched mark): a `refresh` level is asked at once and its rows written in place; any other level is loaded again from its first page, its Loading… page showing until the reply, with the selected row kept wherever the reply now has it: a row the reply adds above it, such as one to continue the film, doesn't take the selection. A `refresh` level is not asked while the run is in front, nor under a game: the ask at the run's end is its next. Send `screen` rows only when the word `screen` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`: older firmware doesn't know the kind and a press does nothing.

A game, or a `screen` row's run, that has the sound (`audio=1`, or `audio=2` while it holds it) can set the Q2's volume, or ask for it, by writing `/tmp/q2-<id>.volume` (whole, by rename): a level from `0` to `100` in decimal, or `?`, with a line end after it or none (six bytes are read). A level is set as the wheel sets it, the Q2's own volume, saved, so the music plays at it afterwards; it is never above 100 nor the user's Maximum volume, and a higher one asked for is set to that limit. Either is answered with a datagram on the game's input socket: `v <level>`, the volume as it now is, and on Bluetooth or a USB DAC a space and what the game's volume argument would be now (`audio=1`, above), so `v 41` on the headphone jack, where the DAC applies it, `v 41 NN` where the game applies the gain `NN` itself (the level, or `100` once a Bluetooth headset applies the level itself) and `v 41 hNN` where a USB DAC does, to show only. The same datagram comes within half a second whenever the level or that argument changes by other means (a headset's volume keys, another output), so a game that listens need not ask twice; the firmware then removes the file, and the level it held can be asked for again. The file is read on every pass of the UI loop while the game has the sound, and its text is acted on once: the same text written again does nothing until the file has held another, or been gone, in between. Text that is neither a level nor `?` is ignored and never answered. Nothing is told at the start: ask with `?` once the input socket is bound, as an answer sent to no socket is lost. The file is not read while the game doesn't have the sound (an `audio=2` game's request is acted on when it takes it), and it is removed when the game starts and when it ends. Firmware that does this lists `volume` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`; older firmware never reads the file, and the game then has its volume argument alone.

A card app can also ask for a sleep timer by writing `/tmp/q2-<id>.sleep` (whole, by rename): `until <unix time>` pauses playback at that time, at most a week away (a time the Q2 slept through by more than a minute is let go), and `end` pauses as the track playing ends (the next one waits at its start). The firmware keeps the timer itself, so it fires after the app has stopped too, and removes the file when it fires or is let go (playback stopping, or another app's timer); the app removes the file to cancel, and a new request replaces its timer. A time is counted from when the firmware reads it, so the clock being set later (over Wi-Fi) doesn't move it. Firmware that does this lists `sleep` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`.

A running card app can set the EQ, or read it, by writing `/tmp/q2-<id>.eq` (whole, by rename): a seq on its first line (1 to 23 letters and digits, then a newline, new for every request), then `get`, `on` (the PEQ plays, 3EQ off), `off` (no EQ; the PEQ is kept) or a preset in the card EQ folder's text (Equalizer APO, at most 16 KB), which plays at once as the EQ page's Apply does. The answer, in `/tmp/q2-<id>.eq.done` within half a second, is `<seq> ok`, then `mode off`, `mode 3eq` or `mode peq` and the PEQ as preset text, or `<seq> error <why>` (`line N: ...` for a preset that doesn't parse) with nothing changed. Send one request at a time and wait for its answer: one replaced before the firmware read it is never answered. Firmware that does this lists `eq` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps` ([internals](internals.md#q2-shelf), EQ).

**Hearts.** A card app can have Now Playing's heart for its songs, so that the heart marks a song a favourite on its server (q2-jellyfin's Jellyfin favourites), when the word `fav` is in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`. There are two kinds of song, and the presses on both reach the app the same way (below).

- A **streamed song**, from the app's URL queue: `/tmp/q2-<id>.urls`, named in its `.play` as `<seq> /tmp/q2-<id>.urls` and written whole (temp + rename), `#Q2URLS/1<TAB>start=<index>` on its first line, then one line a song: `<url or card path><TAB><title><TAB><artist><TAB><album><TAB><cover url><TAB><seconds><TAB><item><TAB><fav>`. `item` is the app's own id for the song (Jellyfin's item id), 1 to 127 bytes, with no tab or line break; `fav` is `1` when the song is a favourite now (a CR after it is taken, for a CRLF file), anything else (empty too) when not. A song whose line has an item has the app's heart: as the song starts the heart shows `fav`, and a tap flips it at once and records the press for the app; stock's Tidal favourite is never called. The firmware keeps each song's heart for the queue, so the presses since show while it plays, and a change the app learns of elsewhere shows only in its next queue. A song of the queue whose line has no item, or one of 128 bytes or more, has no heart: it shows empty, and a tap only says "This app has no favourites" (stock's heart is Tidal's, which can't favourite an app's song); so give every line one, a card path in the queue too (it plays as a local file, but its heart is still the queue's). The same goes for every song of an older app's queue on firmware with the word. Firmware without the word reads six fields and ignores the rest.
- A **synced song**, a plain file the app put on the card for the Library. The app names the folder it syncs into in its `q2app` as `favdir=<name>`: one folder directly in the card's root, without `/` (`favdir=Jellyfin` for `/mnt/mmc/Jellyfin/`); a missing, empty, `.` or `..` value, or one with a `/`, names none. A song under that folder, played from anywhere (the Library, Folder view, a playlist), keeps the Q2's own heart, its favourite (My Fav, five hearts), which shows and changes as for any song. Each change Q2 Pod makes to it is recorded for the app too: Now Playing's heart, five hearts given or taken away in the rating picker, **Add to Favourites** and **Remove from Favourites** in the hold menu, and an album's (each of its songs when it is given hearts; only those that were in My Fav when it is taken away). The heart shows the Q2's own favourite, never the server's. There is no capability word for the key alone: older firmware ignores it, and lists no `fav`.

The presses are lines appended to `/mnt/mmc/.<id>/fav.log`, in the app's own folder, so a press waits there, through a power-off too, until the app runs; the firmware never starts the app for it. Each press appends an LF and then a line

    <kind><TAB><state><TAB><key>

ending in LF, so the file holds empty lines between the presses: the LF before each press ends any line a write cut short, which then never joins the next. `kind` is `item` for a streamed song, `key` then its line's item, or `path` for a synced song, `key` then its full path (`/mnt/mmc/Jellyfin/Broadcast/Tender Buttons/01 - Tears.flac`); `state` is `1` when the song is now a favourite and `0` when it isn't. They come in the order pressed, so the last line for a key is its state; the same state can come twice in a row. The firmware writes each line with one write, appended, without waiting for the card to flush it, and never reads, rewrites or deletes the file. To take the presses, the app renames the file aside in the same folder (`fav.log.taking`, say), waits a second, since a press written as the rename happens goes into the renamed file, reads it, keeps the lines in an outbox of its own until its server has them, and only then deletes the renamed file. It skips empty lines, and drops a line that isn't three fields of `item` or `path`, `0` or `1` and a key, or has no LF at its end (a write the card cut short). It looks at its start and every few seconds while it runs. A press the card doesn't take (no card, full, or read-only) is not recorded: a streamed song's heart then stays as it was and the Q2 says "Favourite not saved"; a synced song's favourite on the Q2 still changes, and the Q2 says "Favourite not sent to the app".

A card app that plays a URL queue (`/tmp/q2-<id>.urls`, named in `.play` as `<seq> /tmp/q2-<id>.urls`; [internals](internals.md#card-apps), Stream mode: URL queues) can be told which of its songs is playing, to report plays to its server as they happen. It asks per queue, with the field `now=1` in the queue's header line, a tab before it: `#Q2URLS/1<TAB>start=<index><TAB>now=1` (fields in any order). While that queue is the one playing, the firmware keeps `/tmp/q2-<id>.now`, one line:

    <seq>	<n>	<state>	<sec>	<length>

- `seq`: the queue's seq, as in the `.play` that named it (up to its first space; at most 23 bytes, cut there). It says which queue the line is about.
- `n`: the song's place in the queue, counted from 0 among the songs the firmware kept, as `start=` counts them. A line it passed over (no URL, a URL that is neither `http://`, `https://` nor a card path, one of 1,024 bytes or more) isn't counted, so for a queue whose every line is a song, `n` is the line's index after the header.
- `state`: `playing`, `paused` or `stopped`.
- `sec`: how far into the song, in whole seconds; `length`: its length in seconds as the player has it, `0` while it doesn't know.

The line never holds the song's URL (which may carry the app's token), only these numbers. The file is written whole (renamed into place) and only when something changes: a new `n` or `state` at once, `sec` at once while paused and after a seek back, and otherwise once it has moved 5 s on while playing (a seek forward of less than 5 s waits for that). While nothing changes, nothing is written. `stopped` is written once, with the `seq`, `n` and `sec` last told, when another queue (a song played from the Library, Tidal, another app's queue) or another player (Spotify, Internet Radio) takes over, when playback stays stopped for 3 s (the moment between two songs doesn't count), or when the next URL queue starts, the app's own or another app's. It is written at once too on **Stop Streaming Services** and on Home's **Rockbox** row, and when the firmware's interface starts again after a crash (or a watchdog restart) with the file left saying `playing` or `paused`: the line is kept and only its state becomes `stopped`. Nothing is written after it until that same queue plays again, which `playing` under the same `seq` says. Nothing is written for a queue that hasn't played yet, nor for one without `now=1`, nor for anything that isn't a URL queue. Only the latest URL queue is told, and only to its own app. The firmware keeps the file whether the app runs or not, so an app started later reads where playback stands; `/tmp` is emptied at power-off, and the app may delete the file (the next change writes it again).

Reading it for a server's play reports (Jellyfin's `/Sessions/Playing`, `/Sessions/Playing/Progress` and `/Sessions/Playing/Stopped`, say): a line whose `seq` or `n` differs from the last one read means the song read before has ended (Stopped, at the position last read for it) and this one has started (Playing); `playing` and `paused` with a new `sec` are progress (Progress, paused or not); `stopped` ends the song at `sec`. A `playing` or `paused` line after a `stopped` one starts a new play of that `n` at its `sec`. A song that starts again under the same `n` (repeat one) reads as a seek back to its start. The app polls the file as often as it likes (it lives in RAM; its modification time moves only with its content). Firmware that does this lists `now` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps`; older firmware ignores the header field and writes no `.now`.

A URL queue's song or cover can be served by the app itself, on `http://127.0.0.1` (a port or a path after it), as q2-jellyfin's relay does so the player never sees the server's token. Firmware that lists `wake` in `/tmp/q2-addon.caps` then starts the app when the player is about to play such a song and the app isn't running (its loop's pid file `/tmp/q2-<id>` gone: it ended idle, or was stopped), as its row starts it, and waits for its browse socket (`/tmp/q2-<id>.browse.sock`) to take a connection, 5 s at most, before the player asks for the song. So an app that serves its queue must be serving its URLs by the time that socket listens, and take a connection that sends nothing (the firmware closes it at once; a bad-request answer is fine). A URL elsewhere, or a card path, starts nothing. A paused song resumes on the connection the player already has, with no new start: keep the server up while a request it serves is open. The queue isn't kept across a reboot. Older firmware starts nothing, and the song fails while the app isn't running.

Statuses: `ok`; `login` (no saved login: pair from the phone); `offline` (Spotify can't be reached); `premium` (the account isn't Premium); `refused` (Spotify refused the login, 401/403); `unavailable` (the item is gone or can't play here, a stale queue position included); `stale` (search's query hash changed, 412: the add-on needs updating); `throttled` (429: the message says when to retry; until then the backend answers `throttled` without asking Spotify); `unsupported` (unknown op); `bad` (malformed or oversized request); `error` (anything else). A temporary failure never removes the saved login.

Ops (`[cursor]` is optional):

| Op               | Arguments                  | Rows                                                                                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `caps`           |                            | `op` rows, one per supported op; `message` is the backend's version, e.g. `q2-v1.3`             |
| `account`        |                            | one `account` row: the user's URI, display name, `Premium`; or status `login`                   |
| `playlists`      | `[cursor]`                 | the user's `playlist` rows                                                                      |
| `playlist`       | `uri [cursor]`             | its `track` rows                                                                                |
| `liked`          | `[cursor]`                 | Liked Songs' `track` rows                                                                       |
| `albums`         | `[cursor]`                 | saved `album` rows                                                                              |
| `artists`        | `[cursor]`                 | followed `artist` rows                                                                          |
| `album`          | `uri [cursor]`             | its `track` rows                                                                                |
| `artist`         | `uri`                      | its top `track` rows, then its `album` rows                                                     |
| `search`         | `category term [cursor]`   | `category` is `tracks`, `albums`, `artists` or `playlists`                                      |
| `play`           | `context [position [uri]]` | none: Q2 becomes the active device and plays                                                    |
| `queue`          |                            | the next `track` rows, `position` its index in this list                                        |
| `skip`           | `position uri`             | none: plays that queue row; `unavailable` if the queue changed                                  |
| `enqueue`        | `uri`                      | none: the track is added to the queue                                                           |
| `settings`       |                            | `setting` rows `shuffle` (`on`/`off`) and `repeat` (`off`/`context`/`track`) as title           |
| `shuffle`        | `on\|off`                  | none                                                                                            |
| `repeat`         | `off\|context\|track`      | none                                                                                            |
| `signout`        |                            | none: playback stops, the saved login is removed, and the backend waits for pairing             |
| `downloaded`     | `[cursor]`                 | complete cached `track` rows, most recently played first; works without Wi-Fi                   |
| `playdownload`   | `uri`                      | none: starts that cached track without requesting its stream URL                                |
| `download`       | `uri`                      | none: idempotently queues a background playlist download; message reports acceptance or failure |
| `downloadstatus` |                            | none: message reports progress or the completed job's result                                    |

Playlist downloads publish `/tmp/q2-librespot.download` atomically, with `downloading=1` while active and `0` when finished, paused, backing off or waiting for a rate-limit cooldown. Q2 Pod keeps the service and power timers alive during the job, without claiming the audio output. Stop Streaming Services or signing out stops the job.
